# HSG PRD Validation — Product, Requirements, and UX Party

- **Reviewers:** 📋 John (Product Manager), 📊 Mary (Business Analyst), 🎨 Sally (UX Designer)
- **Mode:** Validation only; no PRD, addendum, or memlog changes
- **Finding counts:** Critical 0 · High 7 · Medium 7 · Low 0

## Overall verdict

**Promising but not yet decision-ready.** The draft has a clear public purpose, unusually concrete newcomer and healing-prayer intent, sensible launch exclusions, stable functional IDs, and strong non-coercion language. The party nevertheless finds seven high-severity gaps that would force UX, architecture, content operations, or finance to invent policy: the “source of truth” operating contract is unresolved; the launch has no prioritised cut against an unidentified September deadline; content approval and publication are underspecified; sensitive prayer/testimony lifecycles are incomplete; submission channels and duty-of-care behavior are ambiguous; giving promises an export capability that no FR defines while material policy remains open; and the journeys do not cover the product’s high-risk and operational tasks as coherent episodes. The PRD is suitable for focused resolution work, not yet for unqualified implementation handoff.

## Findings

- **[high] The central-source-of-truth promise lacks an operating contract (§1 Vision; §6.2 FR-4; §6.6 FR-17; §9 SM-6; §10 Q3) —** The vision promises “a dependable source of current information,” but Q3 still asks, “What is the source of truth for service schedules and events, and who owns updates?” FR-17 can date and expire content, while SM-6 only says approved changes are published “before the affected service.” Neither defines maximum publish-to-visible time, verified public visibility, correction/escalation ownership, or an emergency unpublish path. The website could return a healthy page with wrong service information and still satisfy the written uptime target. *Fix:* Define authoritative sources and accountable primary/backup owners by content class; define “published” as externally verified public visibility; set freshness and withdrawal/correction targets; and require alerting, reconciliation, and emergency action for critical service/location information.

- **[high] Launch scope is not prioritised against a real deadline or decision owner (§4 Launch Scope; §6 Features and Requirements; §10 Q1 and disposition) —** The launch combines 18 FRs spanning public content, live media, multiple submission flows, testimony governance, volunteer authoring, and one-time payments. Q1 says, “The working target is 15 September; what is the year, and who gives final launch approval?” and the disposition defers it. No must-have/contingent cut, dependency order, capacity assumption, or go/no-go authority exists, so teams cannot trade scope coherently when risk or time changes. *Fix:* Confirm the date, launch approver, and operating owners; label must-ship versus deferrable capabilities; identify dependency gates for sensitive forms, payments, and authoring; and name the evidence that triggers a scope cut.

- **[high] Content governance is assumed by the FRs but deferred as policy (§6.6 FR-16 and FR-17; §9 SM-3; §10 Q2) —** FR-16 says authorised Volunteer Teams can “create, update, preview, and publish the content assigned to them,” while Q2 still asks which teams may draft, review, and publish each type. “Preview” is not bound to the exact revision, references, scheduled state, or production renderer; there is no risk-tiered approval, audit expectation, approval invalidation rule, archive/withdrawal workflow, or urgent-publish procedure. SM-3 measures independence from developers, not safe and correct publication. *Fix:* Add a content-class governance matrix covering draft, review, approval, publish, schedule, archive, emergency unpublish, and audit; require exact resolved preview and approval invalidation after material dependency changes; and test representative publishers on both routine and urgent scenarios.

- **[high] Sensitive prayer, healing-status, and testimony lifecycles are incomplete (§6.3 FR-7 and FR-10; §7 NFR-5; §10 Q4 and Q5) —** The PRD requires visitors to be told “how their information will be used” and says a testimony is not published without consent, but it leaves access, retention, and consent wording open and does not define withdrawal-to-unpublish time, deletion, renewed approval after edits, consent evidence, handling of minors, separation of private contact data from public testimony content, or fail-closed publication when evidence is missing. These omissions prevent safe form design and content workflow design. *Fix:* Define a complete lifecycle per sensitive submission: minimum fields, purpose, recipient roles, consent evidence/version, minor/safeguarding handling, review and publication rules, edit/re-consent triggers, retention/deletion, withdrawal, cache invalidation, and accountable owner; obtain qualified legal/privacy review where required.

- **[high] Prayer/help channel semantics and duty of care are undefined (§6.3 FR-7; §7 NFR-4 and NFR-5; §10 Q4; PRD addendum “Prayer-Request Routing”) —** FR-7 combines a “prayer request” with a request for “practical help,” but does not state whether the channel is monitored continuously, when a response may be expected, what happens for urgent or safeguarding disclosures, or what confirmation means. The addendum routes requests to configured email addresses while recipients, authorised access, privacy handling, and retention remain open. An email-delivery success could be presented as a successful request even when no responsible team can act. *Fix:* Separate or explicitly classify prayer, practical-help, and urgent-risk intents; provide non-emergency and safeguarding guidance; define delivery versus human-response confirmations, monitoring hours/expectations, failed-delivery fallback and escalation; and specify controlled mailbox access, retention, and handoff ownership.

- **[high] The giving contract is internally incomplete (§4.1 “Offerings”; §6.5 FR-14 and FR-15; §10 Q6; PRD addendum “Offerings and Reconciliation”) —** Launch scope promises “anonymous giving, receipts, and finance-team exports,” but FR-14/FR-15 define the payer flow, receipt, and retained record without any export capability or operational finance journey. The addendum assumes reconciliation “using exported data,” while Q6 leaves anonymous-offering, receipt, refund, reconciliation, and audit policies unresolved. “Where permitted” is not an implementable rule, and the definition of a retained reconciliation record is absent. *Fix:* Resolve applicable policy before UX/implementation; add the finance export/reconciliation requirement or remove it from launch scope; define required donor fields, anonymity behavior, record/receipt identifiers, payment-state reconciliation, refund/correction handling, access, retention, audit evidence, and operational acceptance scenarios.

- **[high] The journey set does not provide coherent coverage for the product’s riskiest tasks (§3 UJ-1–UJ-3; §6.5; §6.6; §9 SM-7) —** UJ-3 starts with Meera returning “with a specific purpose” but then makes her check updates, register for events, volunteer, access teachings and prayer timings, and join a Powerhouse in one session. Giving has no end-to-end journey for success, interruption, duplicate/uncertain result, receipt, or anonymous-state choices; Volunteer Team publishing and finance reconciliation have no operational journeys at all. SM-7 then aggregates event, prayer, Powerhouse, serving, and offering into one 85% measure, allowing strong performance on easy tasks to hide failure on a critical one. *Fix:* Split journeys into realistic single-goal episodes with entry trigger, decision points, errors/fallbacks, completion state, and responsible handoff; add operational publisher and finance journeys; and give payment, sensitive submission, and critical content-update journeys independent pass criteria.

- **[medium] Success metrics lack measurement protocols and can mask failure (§7 NFR-1–NFR-3; §9 SM-1–SM-7) —** Targets such as “90% of first-time mobile test participants” and “within three seconds on a typical 4G mobile connection” omit sample size, recruitment criteria, device/browser/assistive-technology coverage, assistance rules, start/end points, percentile or field/lab method, and retest cadence. SM-3 does not define the routine-update scenario corpus, and SM-7 collapses unrelated tasks. *Fix:* Attach a measurement contract to each launch gate: representative audience, minimum task set and sample, environment, start/stop event, assistance/error rule, pass threshold per journey, evidence artifact, owner, and cadence.

- **[medium] Accessibility covers the public surface but not the authoring system that sustains it (§6.6 FR-16; §7 NFR-1; §8.1 Visual Direction; §9 SM-3) —** NFR-1 applies to “Public pages and key journeys,” while operational users must preview and publish dark, image-led content without requirements for keyboard access, screen-reader labels/status, accessible draft/live distinction, alt-text prompting, heading/link checks, or responsive crop/contrast review. An inaccessible or weakly governed authoring workflow can continuously reintroduce public WCAG failures. *Fix:* Add authoring-tool accessibility and accessible-content-support criteria, include representative disabled editors in task testing, and require rendered checks for alt text, structure, focus order, responsive imagery, and text contrast before publication.

- **[medium] The named associated-pastor audience has no differentiated journey, requirement, or measure (§2.1 Primary Audiences; §1 Vision; §3 Key User Journeys) —** Associated pastors are promised “a credible view of the ministry, its current activity, and the right contact points,” but all journeys represent a newcomer, healing seeker, or member. It is unclear whether pastors have a distinct job-to-be-done or are simply another public-content audience. *Fix:* Either add a concise evidence-based pastor journey/outcome and map it to FRs and metrics, or classify associated pastors as a secondary audience served by existing public journeys and remove the implication of bespoke launch scope.

- **[medium] “Languages” is ambiguous between service information and product localisation (§3 UJ-1; §6.1 FR-1; §7 NFR-7) —** Ananya evaluates HSG’s “languages,” and FR-1 says visitors can learn about languages, but the PRD never states whether this means listing languages used in services or providing multilingual website content. The difference materially affects content models, accessibility, SEO, testing, and future regional reuse. *Fix:* State the launch interpretation explicitly, including any required service-language fields and whether UI/content localisation is in or out of scope.

- **[medium] The healing-follow-up-to-offering transition needs a stronger non-coercion contract (§3 UJ-2 “Next step”; §6.3 FR-10; §8.2 Content Tone; §9 SM-C1) —** UJ-2 and FR-10 place a thanksgiving offering directly after reporting healing status or submitting a testimony. The PRD says this is optional and forbids pressure, but it does not define separation, neutral copy, absence of preselection/social proof, or a usability/research test for perceived pressure among vulnerable visitors. *Fix:* Prefer a separate, non-blocking giving path after submission confirmation; prohibit preselected amounts or outcome-linked language; and test whether participants understand that prayer, follow-up, and testimony handling are independent of giving.

- **[medium] Form ownership and data custody vary behind the undefined phrase “approved channel” (§4.2; §6.1 FR-3; §6.3 FR-7 and FR-10; §6.4 FR-12 and FR-13; §7 NFR-4 and NFR-5) —** Event registration is explicitly external, while newcomer, prayer, testimony, Powerhouse, and serving flows may use a “contact method,” “external form,” or “approved channel.” The PRD therefore does not reveal which system collects which fields, owns consent and retention, supplies accessibility/error handling, or produces the “successful submission” confirmation. *Fix:* Create a launch submission-channel matrix by journey, identifying hosted versus external custody, minimum fields, privacy notice/consent owner, confirmation semantics, failure fallback, retention/access, and accessibility responsibility.

- **[medium] Future regional readiness is too abstract to verify (§4.2; §7 NFR-7; §1 Vision) —** NFR-7 says only that “the content structure can support future regional experiences,” while launch is India-only. It does not define which fields are global, region-specific, inheritable, or localisable; how provenance is exposed; or what acceptance evidence proves the structure can expand without leaking or silently inheriting wrong content. *Fix:* Keep regional experiences out of launch, but define the minimum structural acceptance test: stable region identity, explicit global/override provenance for selected representative content, no cross-region authorisation fallback, and a documented expansion path validated in architecture.

## Source reconciliation notes

- **Product brief alignment is strong.** The PRD preserves the central digital-home proposition, the main public audiences, “alive, not brochure-like” current content, youth-forward/dark/mobile direction, official contact aggregation, content-team independence, events, testimonies, prayer, YouTube, offerings, and India-first/future-region intent.
- **Intentional scope changes are traceable.** The memlog records user-approved removal of recurring giving, native event registration, accounts/profiles, and a website-based finance feature; it also records the canonical “Volunteer Team” term, the Sunday healing-card flow, and expanded visit-planning/accessibility needs. These are coherent changes rather than accidental omissions.
- **One source-to-PRD contradiction remains.** The PRD launch scope still promises finance-team exports, and the PRD addendum still assumes exported reconciliation, but the numbered FR set no longer specifies export behavior after the finance feature was removed. This needs an explicit product decision, not an architectural inference.
- **The brief addendum’s unresolved governance concerns remain phase blockers.** Content roles, prayer privacy, testimony consent, giving compliance, media strategy, and observability were carried into §10 or the PRD addendum, but the blanket disposition defers them. Deferral is documented; decision readiness is not achieved.
- **Technical-research requirements were only partially absorbed.** The PRD includes WCAG 2.2 AA, preview, expiry dates, external uptime monitoring, and end-to-end testing, but not exact-revision preview, risk-tiered approval, verified publish-to-public semantics, content freshness/safe-stale targets, testimony withdrawal/minor handling, separate recovery objectives, authoring-tool accessibility, or scoped agent authority. These should become requirements, explicit deferrals with owners/revisit gates, or clearly architecture-owned decisions before the affected work starts.
- **The strongest preservation is ethical.** The PRD retains the source intent to present healing, testimony, and offerings with dignity, adds the guard against promising an individual healing outcome, and names privacy and trust counter-metrics. The remaining work is to turn those principles into observable flow and governance rules.
