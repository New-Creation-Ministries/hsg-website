Fair

This is a product-specific, coherent PRD with credible user journeys, explicit launch exclusions, unusually good counter-metrics, and a clean separation between product requirements and technical handoffs. It is ready to guide early UX exploration, but it is not yet safe as a green-light-to-build contract: several policy and ownership decisions that shape data, payments, publishing, and launch operations remain deferred, while many functional outcomes still depend on subjective words rather than observable completion conditions.

The product/UX and delivery parties found no critical defect and did not recommend restarting the PRD. They did show that downstream teams would have to invent product policy around source-of-truth, sensitive-data, offerings, editorial governance, security, recovery, and launch ownership. UX and architecture discovery may proceed conditionally; implementation-ready stories for those flows should remain blocked until the high findings are resolved.

Decision-readiness

thin

The PRD makes real launch decisions and useful exclusions, but Questions 2–7 defer the content authority model, source of truth, sensitive-data lifecycle, testimony consent, offering policy, monitoring, and incident ownership. Those decisions shape UX, data models, permissions, integration boundaries, estimates, and release approval.

high

Deferred blockers have no decision contract

§10 Questions 1–7
The questions have no owner, decision date, dependency gate, or required evidence; even the target date lacks a year and final approver.
Fix: Record an owner, resolve-by milestone, affected downstream work, required output, and explicit gate for each question.
high

Sensitive-data and offering policies are deferred too late

FR-7, FR-10, FR-14, FR-15; NFR-5
Forms, publishing states, access roles, retention, consent, anonymity, receipts, refunds, and audit evidence cannot be designed safely without a minimum policy contract.
Fix: Define or link authoritative policy before architecture sign-off for the affected flows.

Substance over theater

adequate

The healing-card journey, Powerhouse privacy boundary, one-time offering distinction, external event-registration boundary, editable visit information, and no-specific-healing-outcome metric are distinctly HSG-specific. The principal weak spots are an unbounded future-readiness aspiration and a primary audience that changes no product decision.

medium

Future readiness is unbounded

NFR-7
“Support future regional experiences” is not testable and may invite speculative architecture for out-of-scope regions.
Fix: Remove it from launch scope or define minimum observable region/locale invariants.
low

Associated pastors do not drive a distinct decision

§1; §2.1
No journey, requirement, or metric differs for this named primary audience.
Fix: Add the distinctive outcome or reclassify them as a secondary beneficiary.

Strategic coherence

adequate

The official-digital-home thesis is clear, feature groups follow it, and success metrics test visit planning, healing clarity, content independence, offering integrity, and reliability. The broad launch has no internal priority or principled cut line, and content trust is only partially measured.

medium

Launch scope has no priority or cut line

§4.1; §6
All seven capability groups appear equally launch-critical despite policy, integration, content-readiness, and schedule risk.
Fix: Mark must-ship versus deferrable capabilities and the evidence that triggers each cut.
medium

Content trust is only partially measured

§1; SM-6
Publishing approved changes before a service does not detect incorrect, contradictory, ownerless, or stale critical information.
Fix: Add accuracy and staleness measures for service, location, parking, and healing-card content.

Done-ness clarity

thin

The FR structure is readable and some outcomes are concrete, but subjective terms and incomplete flow states leave downstream teams to invent acceptance rules. Performance, fallback, recovery, and critical-test conditions are also not reproducible.

high

Subjective outcomes lack verifiable completion conditions

FR-1, FR-2, FR-4, FR-8, FR-11, FR-18
“Easy,” “prominent,” “relevant,” “clear,” and “coherent” do not identify what an acceptance test observes.
Fix: Give every FR an observable content, task, state, time/step, or usability consequence.
high

Stateful workflows omit material transitions

FR-3, FR-7, FR-10, FR-12–FR-17
Validation, delivery, duplication, review, approval, denial, publication, expiry, retry, correction, and recovery behavior is incomplete.
Fix: Specify the product-significant happy, failure, denied, duplicate, interrupted, recovery, and expiry states.
medium

NFR measurement contracts are incomplete

NFR-2–NFR-4; NFR-8
“Typical 4G,” “become usable,” “clear fallback,” “protected,” and “critical journeys” are not reproducible definitions.
Fix: Define test profiles, semantic outcomes, recovery objectives, and the authoritative critical-check set.

Scope honesty

adequate

Launch exclusions are explicit, mechanisms are correctly separated into the addendum, and unresolved decisions are visible. The main contradiction is a finance export promised in scope but absent from the FRs; “practical help” also implies a broader service than is defined.

high

Finance export is promised without a functional requirement

§4.1; FR-14–FR-15
Story creation could legitimately omit an export that finance operations and the addendum assume.
Fix: Add an implementation-neutral reconciliation export requirement or remove exports from launch scope.
medium

“Practical help” is an undefined service boundary

§2.3; FR-7
The phrase may imply pastoral care, financial help, emergencies, health crises, or safeguarding duties.
Fix: Define supported categories, response promises, urgent routing, and excluded help types.

Downstream usability

adequate

UJ, FR, NFR, and SM identifiers are stable and extractable, all UJs have named protagonists, and the glossary covers the core domain terms. The main extraction risk is that currentness and publication semantics are not canonical.

medium

Currentness semantics are not canonical

FR-4, FR-6, FR-17, FR-18; SM-6
“Current,” “today,” “next,” “recent,” and “Today” can diverge across UX, scheduling, time zones, livestream state, and tests.
Fix: Define time zone, ordering, expiry, stale state, override behavior, source, and owner per content class.

Shape fit

adequate

The public, multi-audience, UX-heavy shape is appropriate, and the Experience and Tone section earns its place. The operational half of the thesis is underrepresented: non-developer publishing is a launch capability and primary metric, but operational users have no journey.

medium

The load-bearing publishing workflow has no user journey

§2.2; §6.6; SM-3
Receiving a change, finding content, previewing, approval, publishing, verification, correction, and expiry are left to inference.
Fix: Add a concise named operational journey for normal publishing and a time-critical correction.
