# Party Review — Downstream and Delivery Readiness

- **PRD:** `prd.md`
- **Companions reviewed:** `addendum.md`, `.memlog.md`
- **Technical grounding:** `technical-content-driven-ui-and-content-management-patterns-for-the-hsg-website-research-2026-07-14.md`, especially Integration Patterns, Architectural Patterns and Design, Testing and Quality Assurance, Deployment and Operations Practices, Security/Privacy/Accessibility, Risk Assessment, and Appendix B
- **Review mode:** Validation only; no source artifact was edited

## Overall verdict

**Conditionally ready for UX exploration and architecture discovery; not ready as a complete implementation or story-acceptance contract.** The product intent, audience journeys, feature grouping, stable FR identifiers, launch boundaries, tone, and several headline quality targets are strong enough to start low-regret downstream work. However, the PRD explicitly defers decisions that define who may publish or access sensitive data, what “published” and “successful payment” mean, how quickly critical changes must become visible, how recovery is measured, and who owns launch and incidents. Architecture can use those gaps as a bounded decision agenda, UX can explore public-information journeys, and epics can expose dependencies; sensitive-form, testimony, offering, publishing-workflow, and operational stories should not be treated as development-ready until the high findings are resolved.

The review found **0 critical, 8 high, 5 medium, and 0 low** findings.

## Findings

- **[high] Launch approval and incident ownership are unresolved gates (§10 lines 399–407; Addendum §Observability and Release Operations lines 35–39) — The disposition defers all open questions to “future HSG project-team meetings,” while the launch year and final approver remain unknown and monitoring coverage, escalation ownership, and payment-failure reporting remain open. This prevents a testable go/no-go decision and leaves production response without accountable owners. *Fix:* Before architecture sign-off, name the launch date/year, approval authority, primary and backup owners for product/content, privacy, finance, and operations, and define the evidence and incident conditions each owner accepts.

- **[high] The editorial publication contract stops at role labels (§6.6 FR-16–FR-17 lines 272–295; §10 questions 2–3 lines 402–403) — “Authorised Volunteer Teams can create, update, preview, and publish” and “publishing access is limited to authorised people” do not define content-risk classes, draft/review/publish states, separation of duties, approval invalidation, emergency unpublish, audit evidence, or whether “published” means accepted by the authoring tool or verified on the public site. The technical research treats these as architecture-selection inputs, not implementation details. *Fix:* Define an observable workflow per content-risk class: permitted roles/actions, exact revision bound by preview and approval, transition rules, public-verification outcome, audit fields, and emergency correction/unpublish target.

- **[high] Sensitive prayer and testimony data lack a complete lifecycle contract (§6.3 FR-7 and FR-10 lines 172–209; NFR-5 lines 337–342; §10 questions 4–5 lines 404–405; Addendum §Prayer-Request Routing lines 18–21) — The PRD requires notice, consent, HSG approval, and authorised access, but leaves recipients, retention, consent wording, withdrawal, deletion, publication scope, and access decisions open. “Prayer requests will be delivered by email” increases the number of copies and forwarding surfaces without defining custody. Testimony media may also involve minors, yet no guardian or special-handling rule is stated. *Fix:* Define data categories and minimum fields, purpose/notice version, recipient roles, access/audit rules, retention/deletion, consent evidence tied to the approved text/media, minor/guardian handling, withdrawal path, and a maximum withdrawal-to-public-unpublish time across caches and derivatives.

- **[high] Offering success and reconciliation are underspecified for financial integrity (§6.5 FR-14–FR-15 lines 244–268; SM-4 line 383; §10 question 6 line 406; Addendum §Offerings and Reconciliation lines 12–16) — “Payment is confirmed,” “provide a receipt,” “anonymous giving,” and “retained reconciliation record” do not define the authoritative confirmation source, duplicate/replayed/late result behavior, retry safety, receipt identity/delivery, refund/cancellation policy, export completeness, retention, audit evidence, or reconciliation exception ownership. The provider choice of Razorpay does not resolve these product outcomes. *Fix:* Specify the offering state model and observable invariants: one authoritative provider-confirmed result, idempotent handling of duplicate/late notifications, no false-success screen or duplicate record, receipt and export fields, anonymous-giving policy boundary, reconciliation exception workflow, retention/audit needs, and owner-approved refund/correction rules.

- **[high] Quality targets do not yet form measurable SLO and recovery contracts (§7 NFR-3–NFR-4 lines 325–335; SM-5–SM-6 lines 384–388; §10 question 7 line 407) — “Usable within three seconds on a typical 4G mobile connection” lacks named pages, device/network profile, percentile, measurement method, and window. The 99.9% target names external monitoring but not probe locations/frequency, semantic success, or owner. “Before the affected service” is not a maximum publish-to-visible or safe-stale threshold, and no RTO/RPO or restore-success criteria exist for code, content, records, assets, or secrets. *Fix:* Define page/journey-specific performance measurement, a semantic availability SLI, publish-to-visible and maximum-safe-stale targets by content class, plus separate RTO/RPO/retention and witnessed-restore requirements for each recoverable stream.

- **[high] The content model and editorial autonomy boundary are not decision-ready (§6.6 FR-16 lines 274–284; §8.1 line 365; Addendum §Content Administration lines 30–33) — Routine content spans structured records, pages, testimonies, media, and replaceable backgrounds, but the PRD does not say which are fixed semantic templates, which pages allow governed composition, what fields and references are required, or what combinations/nesting are permitted. The administration model remains open while SM-3 expects 90% of routine scenarios to succeed without developers. These gaps materially change editor UX, accessibility, validation, test state space, and platform fit. *Fix:* Inventory the routine update scenarios and classify each content type as fixed template, governed block/slot, or non-composable; define required metadata, permitted composition boundaries, preview expectations, and representative editor acceptance tasks before selecting the admin architecture.

- **[high] Sources of truth and integration failure semantics are unresolved for time-critical content (§6.2 FR-4–FR-6 lines 138–166; §10 questions 3 and 8 lines 403 and 408; Addendum §Integration Candidates lines 23–28) — Service schedules/events have no named authoritative source or field-level owner; Calendar, YouTube, forms, and social are candidates rather than contracts. The PRD says the site is a reliable current source but does not define cancellation/timezone behavior, last-known-good content, blocked embeds, stale live status, or provider-outage fallbacks beyond the broad NFR-4 phrase “a clear fallback or next step.” *Fix:* For each external dependency, name authoritative fields, freshness and reconciliation expectations, degraded public behavior, user-visible staleness/cancellation rules, and the owner who corrects drift; preserve vendor choice for architecture.

- **[high] Security requirements lack verifiable negative boundaries (§7 NFR-5 lines 337–342; §6.6 lines 276–283; Addendum §§Platform, Prayer Routing, and Integration Candidates) — “Accessible only to authorised people” and “publishing access is limited” omit named identity classes, least-privilege actions, strong-authentication requirements, preview/draft isolation, audit scope, abuse/rate controls for public submissions, credential separation, revocation, and security response ownership. Sensitive email delivery, editable embeds/media, admin publishing, payment callbacks, and future automation each expose different trust boundaries. *Fix:* Add platform-neutral security acceptance requirements that identify principal classes and allowed/denied actions, require named identities and stronger authentication for privileged roles, separate public/draft/management data, audit privileged changes without logging sensitive content, constrain uploads/embeds, rate-limit abuse-prone submissions, and define credential revocation and compromise response.

- **[medium] The release-test gate names categories but not the critical test set (§7 NFR-8 lines 353–356; Addendum line 39) — “Critical journeys covering content, forms, media, and offerings” and “a critical end-to-end check” are circular until the critical cases, environments, fixtures, data cleanup, and pass/fail evidence are enumerated. End-to-end checks alone also cannot economically cover authorization, schedule boundaries, duplicate callbacks, content variants, and degraded dependencies. *Fix:* Define the launch-critical journey/risk matrix and required evidence, including happy, failure, denied, duplicate, interrupted, stale, accessibility, and recovery cases; let test architecture choose the appropriate unit, contract, component, browser, and manual layer for each.

- **[medium] External-form and email handoffs have incomplete user outcomes (§6.1 FR-3 line 132; §6.3 FR-7 lines 172–180; §6.4 FR-12–FR-13 lines 224–242; Addendum §Prayer-Request Routing lines 18–21) — Several journeys rely on an “approved external” form or contact method, while only FR-7 requires successful confirmation. The PRD does not define what happens when a handoff is unavailable, abandoned, duplicated, blocked, or accepted by the website but not delivered to the responsible team. *Fix:* Define the user-visible success/failure/fallback outcome, ownership handoff, duplicate behavior, privacy notice responsibility, and delivery/receipt evidence required for every external submission channel.

- **[medium] Volunteer editor usability is measured without defining the scenarios or admin accessibility baseline (§2.2; FR-16–FR-17 lines 272–295; NFR-1 lines 313–317; SM-3 line 382) — NFR-1 applies to “public pages and key journeys,” not explicitly to the authoring experience on which developer independence depends. SM-3 has a percentage target but no scenario set, representative Volunteer Team roles, assistance definition, sample, error threshold, or recovery expectations. *Fix:* Define the representative editor tasks and participants, completion/error/help criteria, training assumptions, and an accessible keyboard/error/status baseline for the selected authoring workflow.

- **[medium] Future regional readiness is too abstract to guide today’s content structure (§4.2 line 82; NFR-7 lines 349–351) — “Support future regional experiences” does not distinguish region from language/locale, identify which content can be shared or must remain India-specific, or require visible provenance and safe fallback. Even without building another region now, these choices affect IDs, contacts, events, testimonies, permissions, and consent scope. *Fix:* State the minimum future-ready invariants: region and locale are separate dimensions; India-owned legal/contact/event data does not silently inherit elsewhere; shared/override provenance is explicit; and future variants can be introduced without exposing another region’s drafts or private data.

- **[medium] Success metrics lack execution metadata (§9 lines 376–395) — Several targets are useful, but the PRD does not consistently state sample size, sampling/retest cadence, measurement owner, evidence source, observation window, or what counts as assistance. Absolute claims such as “Every confirmed offering” and “No sensitive ... information is exposed” need continuous controls and audit evidence rather than a one-time usability study. *Fix:* Add an owner, measurement method/data source, cohort or denominator, cadence/window, and decision threshold for each metric; distinguish pre-launch usability tests, release gates, and ongoing production controls.

## Downstream-readiness notes

### UX

Proceed with the information architecture, visual system, newcomer/visit-planning journeys, “Today” experience, responsive navigation, and content-tone exploration. Treat prayer/testimony capture, offering, consent, external handoffs, editor workflows, and all failure/denied states as decision-dependent tracks. UX sign-off for those tracks should wait for the corresponding policy and lifecycle contracts, because form fields, notice text, confirmation, error recovery, and role screens depend on them.

### Architecture

Proceed with architecture discovery and evidence-gathering, not a final platform/topology commitment. The immediate architecture agenda is product-level: content composition boundaries; role/publication semantics; source-of-truth mapping; sensitive-data lifecycle; offering state invariants; freshness/staleness; recovery objectives; and security/operations ownership. Supabase and Razorpay are intended directions in the addendum, while hosting, admin model, and several integrations remain open; architecture should preserve that status and use the technical research’s common vertical slice to qualify options.

### Epics and stories

Epics may be created now if they preserve the unresolved decisions as explicit blocking enablers and do not convert open policy into accidental implementation. Development-ready stories can safely begin around low-risk public information and design-system foundations once UX defines their acceptance states. Stories for prayer/help submissions, testimony publication/withdrawal, offerings, editor roles/publication, live/current-content reconciliation, backup/restore, and launch operations should remain blocked or clearly provisional until the related high findings are resolved.

### Test architecture

Risk analysis and a draft test strategy can begin immediately. A final launch gate cannot yet be approved because “critical” checks, semantic uptime, freshness, recovery, negative authorization, privacy lifecycle, and offering idempotency are not sufficiently specified. The first useful artifact is a risk-to-evidence matrix that turns each high finding into observable acceptance and release evidence.
